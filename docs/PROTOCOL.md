# Protocol 4: conversation, input grants, and rendered speech

All four sockets authenticate with protocol version 4: `control`, `audio` (microphone/relay), `video`, and `judge` (Bonk PCM). The first three are required for connection readiness; judge-channel failure repairs independently and cannot stop healthy friend conversation. Incompatible clients receive the existing refresh instruction. No player recordings are stored. Authentication binds commands and media to the reserved seat and current connection epoch.

## Microphone ownership and binary format

All integers and PCM samples are little-endian. Microphone frames have a 31-byte header followed by signed int16 mono PCM at 16 kHz. Normal frames contain 320 samples (20 ms); partial frames are valid, with a defensive maximum of 1,600 samples. `health` may have no samples; an end marker must have no samples.

| Offset | Field                                                           |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0      | type u8 = 1                                                     |
| 1      | media epoch u32                                                 |
| 5      | monotonically increasing socket sequence u32                    |
| 9      | rate u32 = 16000                                                |
| 13     | sample count u32                                                |
| 17     | first capture timestamp f64 in synchronized server milliseconds |
| 25     | purpose u8: social=0, gameplay=1, private=2, health=3           |
| 26     | input epoch u32 (zero for social/health)                        |
| 30     | end marker u8: 0 or 1                                           |

The worklet tags samples when captured and flushes partial PCM before changing purpose/epoch. Receipt time never changes their purpose. Authentication supplies player and connection identity. Server eligibility additionally requires current floor ownership, input epoch, capture window, and matching immutable transcription turn. Off-turn speech, private tests, and stale grants cannot produce shared transcripts, claims, scores, or Bonk cancellation. The provider may receive generated zero samples to finish turn detection; these do not contain off-turn audio.

Capture refreshes its audio-context/server-clock mapping every 250 ms while running. A clock refresh flushes the partial frame under its original timestamp and epoch before updating the mapping; it never emits a turn end or retimestamps queued audio. This prevents audio-clock drift or a device interruption from making otherwise fresh relay audio appear stale. The same refreshed clock closes the worklet's capture deadline.

Conversation routing is independent: RTC carries unmuted friend audio throughout every phase; relay forwards fresh social/gameplay PCM regardless of floor. Explicit mic mute disables the source track; friend mute affects local playback only. Private checks detach the RTC sender, close the conversation capture gate, and silence incoming friend playback before prompting. Health packets establish capture continuity without uploading off-turn PCM in RTC mode. Relay uses the same 750 ms capture-age admission budget at the server and receiving client. Its 80 ms prebuffer and bounded jitter buffer remain independent of this budget; packet admission does not establish acoustic delivery.

Each seat has microphone token buckets allowing a burst of 200 messages and 128 KiB, refilling at 100 messages and 40 KiB per second. Byte accounting includes the binary header. Socket replacement and seat resume retain the budget. Capture freshness remains three seconds; epoch, sequence, purpose and capture-window guards still apply. Exhaustion fails a matching private check, or safely aborts an unfinished match when eligible gameplay audio or its end marker would be lost. Social audio may be discarded. The authenticated diagnostics response adds fixed counters for rejection reasons without storing media.

Relay friend playback prebuffers 80 ms to absorb browser message-delivery jitter. Backlog beyond 160 ms is reduced to the newest 80 ms, keeping degraded conversation responsive. These bounds apply only to conversational relay audio; judge playback never skips samples.

## Turn coordination

`floor_granted` assigns ownership with `inputState: preparing` and a paused clock. The room can prepare the successor’s immutable provider session while the previous turn settles. No idle sessions remain, and the pool holds at most four live/draining sessions. Once both devices, the matching provider, settlement, and Bonk completion are ready, `input_granted` publishes a new room-owned epoch with no running deadline. The first valid capture frame, including silence, opens the turn and emits `floor_opened`. Its capture timestamp, bounded by grant time and server receipt, anchors the authoritative clock. Three seconds without first capture enters recovery.

Only Done or the deadline ends an opportunity. Provider silence/turn-end signals never yield the floor. Done is acknowledged locally at once; the worklet flushes the current partial frame and sends an end marker behind eligible PCM on the same ordered audio socket. The control `done.lastAudioSeq` identifies that marker. Control and audio may arrive in either order. `turn_sealed.capturedBefore` retains the exact capture deadline even if a server tick runs late; trailing out-of-window samples are excluded. A Done that races an already sealed turn is acknowledged without changing state. At the deadline the worklet closes at the sample boundary. Settlement drains through the marker immediately, with a three-second missing-marker bound, then awaits provider close/final events (five seconds) and scoring (existing fifteen-second settlement limit). Failure aborts honestly; it cannot advance by discarding accepted evidence. A clock never runs during input preparation, judge speech, sealing, or recovery.

When Bonk intervenes, the current grant closes at a server capture-time cutoff. Its epoch remains identifiable during preparation so in-flight pre-cutoff samples and an ordered end marker can arrive for up to three seconds; post-cutoff samples remain ineligible. This also lets a Done already in flight settle normally. Resuming requires a fresh grant and epoch.

## Judge generation and playback

Fish emits native int16 mono 44.1 kHz PCM. The server repackets arbitrary provider chunks into 882-sample (20 ms) frames plus a final partial frame. The separate judge socket uses a 17-byte header: type u8 at 0 (2=PCM, 5=EOF), speech epoch u32 at 1, sequence u32 at 5, rate u32 at 9 (=44100), and absolute sample offset u32 at 13. EOF has no payload and its offset is the final sample count. Sequence/offset must be contiguous. Encoder and decoder share the same size limits.

The client AudioWorklet queues approximately 120 ms before `audio_ready`. After both authenticated seats are ready, the server publishes a shared future `startAt`, with lead `max(150 ms, min(1000 ms, max seat RTT + 50 ms))`. Cached delivery is paced no more than 400 ms ahead and checks socket backpressure. Each utterance is bounded to 882,000 samples; fixed voice cache reserves at most six clips (under 12 MiB). Rebuffering inserts silence without skipping or speeding up remaining samples.

`audio_ready` and `audio_playback` are idempotent command envelopes with utterance ID and speech epoch; the authenticated socket supplies seat/connection ownership. Playback statuses are `progress`, `complete`, or `failed`, with cumulative samples. Progress reports normally arrive every 250 ms. Provider EOF ends generation only. Normal completion requires both clients to consume the exact final sample count, drain output, and send a plausible completion for the current utterance and connection. Duplicate/stale acknowledgments cannot complete another line. Bonk mute changes gain, not render accounting.

The render thread supplies its final sample’s audio-context time. The client waits for `getOutputTimestamp().contextTime` to pass it when available; otherwise it waits reported output/base latency plus a conservative 250 ms guard on the running audio clock. This is browser output timing, not proof of acoustic output. Mouth motion uses local rendered audio, not server scheduling. Captions remain visible until shared completion, independently of voice success.

Failure bounds per attempt: five seconds to first generated audio; three seconds without generation/render progress; twenty seconds of audio or synthesis; thirty seconds overall. Complete-sentence copy limits replace old 3/4.5/6/8-second sample truncation. A recoverable delivery interruption may replay the entire cached utterance once after a repair bounded to three seconds. Further failure or an incomplete source uses the complete shared caption for 2–6 seconds based on length. `captions` is distinct from `silent` and `speaking`. Normal navigation discards obsolete queued cues but lets active speech finish. Leave, abort, room disposal, and genuine device/connection recovery explicitly cancel owned speech; cancellation alone cannot open a floor.

## ICE and telemetry

`POST /api/ice` requires room/player/token/current connection epoch and is rate-limited. With `TURN_URLS` and `TURN_SECRET`, it returns ten-minute TURN REST credentials scoped to that seat/epoch, plus STUN. Responses are `no-store`. The client refreshes before new negotiation if ownership changed or credentials are expiring. Without configuration it uses STUN and the existing WSS fallback; provisioning TURN remains deployment work.

`POST /api/diagnostics` requires the current seat credentials, is rate-limited, and returns only the bounded timeline and aggregate samples with `no-store`. No audio recordings are collected. The room retains at most 512 metadata events and 128 samples per latency category. Stages include input grant/first capture/seal, provider final/drained, score commit, first TTS packet/EOF, and each client’s render progress/completion. `playback_metric` identifies the utterance and supplies render-clock latency, cue-to-render, and Done-to-render; the server rejects stale ownership and tags transport/cache state. These client measurements are diagnostic estimates, never scoring authority. See [measurement and qualification](AUDIO_ACCEPTANCE.md).

## Setup

Create/join reserves a seat before device setup. `setup_start` supplies a unique `checkId` and the current microphone track `captureId`. Retries reuse the seat. `setup_cancel` cancels the check; `/api/leave` can release a seat even before the control socket opens, with token and connection-epoch validation. Late reservation responses release their obsolete seat. Device acquisition is single-flight: one abortable attempt owns new tracks and audio nodes within a 30-second deadline. Cancellation before audio unlock completes cannot start a permission request. Late permission responses stop only their newly returned tracks.

Replacement tracks and their audio graph are initialized before the active stream changes. Failure, cancellation, or timeout leaves borrowed healthy tracks and the previous graph intact. Successful worklet loading is cached per audio context; failed loads may retry. Cancelling the subsequent private speech check retains committed capture; leaving stops all capture. These ownership types remain client-internal. Mic and camera privacy flags belong to the player and are initialized only for a new seat. Replacement tracks inherit the latest choices before publication, including choices made during permissions or module loading. Muting during a private check suspends that check without a device-error message; capture repair may finish, but the game stays paused. Explicit re-enabling continues checks with healthy capture and valid speech proof retained.

Playback initialization has one attempt owner and a 10-second deadline, including downloads and their response bodies. The audio-unlock deadline remains two seconds. Peer-playback, judge-playback, and capture worklet sources are fetched with their owner's abort signal, then evaluated through an attempt-owned Blob URL; this avoids the browser retaining a stalled `addModule` download across retries. These worklets must remain self-contained. Successful module registration is reusable, but only the current attempt can publish new playback nodes. Cancellation, timeout, or failure disconnects staged nodes and releases the attempt. Setup cancellation and Leave cancel pending initialization without discarding a healthy audio context or working playback. Loading failures say “Couldn’t load sound. Try again.”; permission failures retain device-specific help.

The browser unlocks Web Audio from the initiating tap, acquires both devices, and uses the normal 16 kHz PCM worklet and authenticated audio socket. The server opens a private transcription session with no turn ID. Once the provider confirms its connection, `setup_result: listening` starts the 15-second speech window. Nonzero captured PCM plus a nonempty final provider transcript produces `setup_result: passed`. Interim captions and setup transcripts never enter shared captions, claims, judging, or the ledger. Results go only to the tested seat and carry the check ID plus a room-issued `inputEpoch` while listening. Before the speech prompt, the client awaits a capture-worklet isolation acknowledgment and RTC sender detachment; incoming friend playback is muted and flushed. The server rejects forwarding to or from a private check. Restore the latest privacy settings afterward.

The transcription controller selects an internal purpose for each provider session: idle, private check, or gameplay turn. A pending private check takes precedence over the paused floor holder during socket authentication, provider rotation, and recovery. Its immutable owner includes the check ID, microphone capture identity, and connection epoch. Private PCM is sent only to that matching, connected session; private callbacks can update readiness only. Obsolete connections, cancelled checks, replaced microphones, and departed seats cannot publish private results. Legitimate final evidence from an earlier gameplay session may still drain through its existing turn boundary.

Socket reconnection retains an unfinished check's ID and original deadlines. Listening and captured-audio proof are cleared until the replacement private provider connects. The first provider connection opens the 15-second speech window once; subsequent connections and duplicate requests cannot extend it or the match's cumulative recovery allowance. The client silences its check input while reconnecting, then repeats `setup_start` with the same ID after authentication and a fresh health report. The server retains only the latest private result, bounded to 300 characters, and replays it without creating another provider or rerunning speech. A saved success can be replayed after play resumes; a saved failure remains a failure. Cancellation, source failure, and capture replacement clear obsolete results. Private packets must match the current check input epoch; a delayed check packet can never become gameplay speech.

Permission requests time out after 30 seconds; room/media and provider connections after 10 seconds. A connected speech test times out after 15 seconds. Cancellation closes the check session and invalidates queued retries. Successful checks persist while the same capture pipeline remains healthy. Device replacement or capture/provider failure invalidates the affected proof. Manual privacy toggles preserve the successful speech test but block play.

## Health and delivery

The client reports `media_status` every second: microphone/camera identity, source health distinct from manual mute, foreground state, advancing local/remote video counters, camera generation, media epoch, and RTC connection/failure state. The server combines those observations with actual recent microphone packets, provider state, successful private speech proof, and video receipts. A client cannot pass readiness by sending `ready: true` in a status message.

Video packets use a 21-byte header: type (3 for peer video, 4 for probe), media epoch, sequence, capture timestamp, and camera generation, followed by JPEG bytes. Probes use the existing authenticated video socket; the server echoes them to their sender. A receipt is sent only after a successful decode and generation check. Peer relay receipts likewise follow decoding. Receipts must reference a fresh frame actually sent to that destination, with the current epoch. Device replacement invalidates prior echo and reciprocal delivery proof. Packets older than three seconds, future timestamps, replayed sequences, wrong generations, and obsolete connections are rejected.

WebRTC uses advancing inbound decoder counts on a live receiver track; local capture uses video frame callbacks plus decoder counters, deduplicated against the same preview's frame count. An empty preview or delayed callback for a cached frame cannot invent progress. Offscreen previews therefore do not imply capture failure. RTC playback uses the same unlocked Web Audio gain path as relay audio; video elements remain muted for mobile autoplay. The first seat opens negotiation to avoid initial offer collisions. An explicit RTC failure selects the existing WebSocket fallback. A connection that remains open but stops delivering decoded frames also switches to relay after three seconds of delivery blockage, provided both captures are healthy. Recovery stays paused until the replacement transport delivers fresh frames and passes the same stability checks.

Explicit failures block immediately; local observations do not wait for RTC statistics. Missing progress expires after three seconds. Both players need two healthy observations at least one second apart and completed transport selection before becoming ready. Qualification also requires local capture counters, incoming decoded delivery, and decoded camera echoes to advance beyond the first observation. Repeated reports within one cached frame's freshness window cannot qualify. Checkpoints reset on renewed failure, device generation, connection epoch, and media epoch changes. RTC senders and receiving gains stay connected across turns and Bonk speech. In RTC mode social capture uploads only health metadata; relay mode uploads social PCM. No idle transcription session is required. Planned provider rotation does not reset otherwise healthy camera/capture qualification. Capture remains alive across handoffs; the manual privacy switch disables its source track. Client observations are still a browser trust boundary, not hardware attestation.

A camera repair explicitly requests a replacement video track even if the stalled track still reports `live`. The healthy microphone, capture graph, and speech proof remain. Failed or cancelled acquisition cannot replace the current stream; late tracks are stopped. Manual privacy toggles reuse the existing capture, and delivery-only failures retain transport recovery without requesting devices again. These repair intents are client-internal.

A refreshed page restores the seat first and asks for one tap to restart devices and unlock audio. Socket-only reconnects reuse the healthy capture stream and speech proof.

## Connection lifecycle

Restoration and socket reconnection share one active connection attempt. Each HTTP request, response body, and socket handshake has a combined 10-second deadline. Retries are serial with 1, 2, then 4 seconds of backoff, capped at 4 seconds. Automatic attempts stop at the remaining known recovery deadline, or after 60 seconds if it is unavailable. Later snapshots can shorten that deadline, never extend it. Manual retry can retrieve an expired match's final state; it does not reset the server allowance. Leave aborts requests, handshakes, and backoff; obsolete responses cannot attach sockets.

`POST /api/resume` accepts the existing token, seat identity, and `connectionEpoch`, plus an optional UUID `resumeId`. New clients persist the source session and a fresh ID together before sending each logical resume. HTTP retries and restoration after a lost response reuse that ID. An accepted response atomically replaces the stored session and clears its pending ID before opening sockets.

A request at the current epoch advances once and records the transition’s ID, source epoch, and resulting epoch. A retry from the recorded source epoch must supply that same ID; it returns the current session and snapshot without closing channels, changing media epochs, or emitting another resume event. Distinct IDs, unidentified old epochs, older transitions, future epochs, and reuse of the last ID for a different transition return 409 without disturbing current sockets. Epoch-only clients may resume at the current epoch. Each new transition, including a legacy one, supersedes the previous transition’s retry/cancellation authority. Protocol 4 clients and servers must deploy together.

Connection loss immediately replaces the local task with recovery, closes judge input, and blocks gameplay commands while preserving mounted media and scores. A successful HTTP response alone cannot restore the task: the client must receive an authenticated control snapshot, synchronize its clock, and open all three required sockets. Server readiness then governs whether the floor can resume. If automatic retries end, the interface says “Connection lost. Check your network.” and offers “Retry connection” alongside the existing Leave control.

## Deliberate departure

Authenticated HTTP Leave and the socket `leave` command delegate to the same room policy. Leaving an unfinished match aborts it before releasing the seat, cancelling match-owned judging, speech, handoffs, and timers. Seat ownership and transcription bookkeeping are revoked before providers or sockets close. Late evidence, provider completions, and socket closures cannot restart recovery or change the terminal result. Repeating room cleanup is harmless. `/api/leave` accepts the same optional `resumeId`: current-epoch departure uses normal token authentication, while departure from the previous epoch requires ownership of the latest recorded resume transition. The client captures these credentials before cancelling its connection attempt. This releases a seat even when the server accepted resume but its response was lost. A newer transition revokes that exception; stale clients cannot evict the replacement. Leaving before resume reaches the server removes the seat, so late resume cannot recreate it. Repeated HTTP departure after seat removal returns 401 without further state changes.

Lobby cancellation only releases the seat. A completed match retains participant records for its winner, scores, and verdict, while removing the departing player's authenticated seat and readiness. An interrupted room with fewer than two players shows the named departure reason and “Back to menu”, with no recovery countdown or device repair. Completed results retain their verdict and offer the same exit when the other player has gone. Protocol 4 clients and servers must deploy together.

## Game guards and recovery

Ready, match creation, rematch, recovery completion, and floor resume all consult server-owned capability state. Invalidating a capability clears ready/rematch commitments. Each replacement speaking-turn transcription session must connect before the floor opens, including recovery from a private check. Planned provider transitions hold the clock without spending the device-recovery allowance unless they fail or exceed their connection deadline.

The registry checks the single-match admission limit synchronously at the actual match-start transition, including starts deferred behind judge playback. If another match occupies the stage, the completed verdict remains intact, ready/rematch votes clear, and both players receive the existing stage-occupied explanation. They can vote again once the stage is free.

Transcription drainage succeeds only after a requested, error-free provider close with code 1000, no unsent messages, and consumption of queued final events. Provider errors, abnormal closes and the five-second drain deadline fail settlement; subsequent evidence is ignored. Failed transports are forcibly disposed, and remain in active resource accounting until physical closure completes. Abort retires current, draining and prepared gameplay sessions, while permitted private checks retain their isolation. `/healthz` and authenticated diagnostics count active transcription transports, including connecting and draining sessions.

Recovery keeps separate blockers for each player and capability. Clearing one cannot clear another. Phase, floor holder, remaining time, score, accepted evidence, queued judgments, and the prior pause are retained. Cancelled judge playback cannot later open a stale floor. Recovery resumes only after both players are healthy, within the existing cumulative 60-second allowance. Sealed evidence still drains and settles; failure to finalize it aborts safely rather than silently dropping evidence. The typed-argument command, typed evidence source, and STT degradation mode are removed. Bonk's existing captions-only voice fallback remains.

## Bonk feedback

`bonk_reaction` links an ID, player, round, turn, and text to an accepted positive score award. The server uses the first suitable reason per turn, at least six seconds apart, while Bonk is silent. Copy is at most eight words and 80 characters. Long/unsuitable reasons keep their normal score award without an aside. No extra provider request is made. Spoken handoffs exclude verbatim shown reasons.

The client verifies award ownership, exact reason, current match/round/turn, fresh sequence, and unused reaction identity. It displays one silent aside for 3.2 seconds with no backlog. Snapshots seed the event cursor; reconnects and remounts never replay historical rewards. Speech, recovery, reset, and visibility changes clear the presentation. The authoritative ledger, fractional score units, 400ms retargetable total, and collision-free award queue are unchanged.
